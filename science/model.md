# Global equilibrium thermometry with finite feedback

[Science index](README.md) · [Entropy budget](entropy_budget.md) · [Converse](two_batch_converse.md)

## Parameter and loss

The unknown reservoir temperature $T$ is known only to lie in a fixed, positive, nondegenerate interval $[T_-,T_+]$. Write

$$
x=\ln T,\qquad a=\ln T_-,\qquad b=\ln T_+,\qquad 0<b-a<\infty.
$$

The reported estimate is $\widehat x=\ln\widehat T$. We evaluate **global minimax squared log-temperature error**

$$
R_r^*(N;[a,b])=
\inf_{\mathcal P_r,\widehat x}
\sup_{x\in[a,b]}
\mathbb E_x[(\widehat x-x)^2].
$$

The infimum ranges over all admissible $r$-batch policies and estimators with no more than $N$ thermalized qubit uses on every branch. Truncating $\widehat x$ to $[a,b]$ does not increase the error, so the estimator may be assumed range-valued. There is no local-unbiasedness requirement.

For fixed $T_\pm$, an equivalent ordinary squared relative-temperature risk has the same asymptotic exponents, since $\exp$ and $\ln$ are bi-Lipschitz on the compact interval. We use the logarithmic loss because thermal likelihoods have a natural Fisher metric in $x$ and because it does not privilege a temperature scale.

## Gibbs-preparation contract

Given a selected Hermitian Hamiltonian $H$ on $n$ qubits, one paid thermalization returns the full Gibbs state

$$
\rho_{H,x}=\frac{\exp(-e^{-x}H)}{\mathrm{Tr}\exp(-e^{-x}H)}.
$$

The Hamiltonian is fixed independently of the **unknown** $x$ once selected. It may have arbitrary, many-body interactions and arbitrarily chosen energy levels, subject to $n$ being charged. Adding a constant multiple of the identity changes no thermal probability. In the upper-bound construction $H$ is explicitly nonnegative.

Crucially, thermalization means **fresh replacement** by $\rho_{H,x}$: no unknown input correlations with retained ancillary systems persist through the operation. If a thermostat channel retains input information, directly exposes its environment, or allows coherent control over its temperature-dependent dynamics, it is not this access model.

A batch may contain multiple separately prepared Gibbs blocks and subsequent arbitrary quantum processing. The batch's list of block Hamiltonians, sizes, and any preselected randomization must be fixed before its outputs are observed. Equivalently, several commuting blocks can be regarded as the single Gibbs state of a Hamiltonian acting on all charged qubits.

Between batches the experimenter may inspect a classical instrument record, change the next Hamiltonian, reallocate remaining qubits and choose a final measurement. No free preliminary calibration is given. A decision based on any record produced **during** the next preparation batch counts as an additional feedback opportunity if it changes that batch's Hamiltonian.

## Allowable measurements and memory

Quantum instruments, joint POVMs, temperature-independent ancillas, and quantum memories carried from earlier batches are permitted. The following statistical sufficiency observation is important for the **converse**.

For fixed $H$, choose its orthonormal energy eigenbasis $\{|i\rangle\}$ and write

$$
\rho_{H,x}=\sum_i p_H(i|x)|i\rangle\langle i|.
$$

Each index $i$ is a sufficient *refinement*: the actual Gibbs state can be generated by first sampling the classical energy-basis index $i$ with probability $p_H(i|x)$ and then preparing $|i\rangle$, independently of $x$. Any instrument, including one retaining a quantum memory, can be simulated by an $x$-independent quantum channel acting on that prepared index state. Degenerate eigenspaces can be resolved in any fixed basis; the extra labels have $x$-independent conditional distribution.

Reveal all indices to a hypothetical stronger controller. It can reproduce any original instrument and its classical feedback, while also possessing the finer energy labels. Conditional on its classical history, the next selected Hamiltonian is independent of the unknown parameter. The eventual quantum-memory measurement is then a parameter-independent channel of the classical indices and recorded choices. Thus, by classical data processing, it suffices to lower-bound the risk of adaptive **energy-label experiments**.

This reduction is valid for any state that is a full, known-basis Gibbs replacement. It is **not** a theorem about arbitrary partially thermalizing dynamical channels, temperature-dependent eigenbases, inaccessible control parameters, or a free bath output.

## Resources and scope

- The integer $N$ is a **pathwise** cap: any branch, including failed calibration records, consumes at most $N$ qubits. Expected-budget policies with rare enormous batches are different.
- For two rounds, $H_1$ is fixed before observations; $H_2(Y)$ may depend on the complete first classical record $Y$. The second batch has no subsequent retuning.
- The lower bound allows arbitrary within-batch many-body Hamiltonians, quantum processing, biased estimators, and first-to-second quantum memory under the Gibbs-replacement contract. It need not assume the product form used by the construction.
- The upper bound uses ordinary fixed-gap calibration qubits and independently prepared, energy-degenerate many-body blocks. It makes no hardware preparation-time, locality or coupling-strength guarantee.
- Numerical constants may depend on $T_\pm$ and on fixed selected gaps, but not on $N$. The asymptotic statements concern $N\to\infty$ at fixed prior interval.
